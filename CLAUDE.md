## Research question

> **Are superstars more self-focused than role players?**

Framing: does a person's social status show up in the way they talk? Lab studies (Kacewicz, Pennebaker, Davis, Jeon & Graesser, 2014, "Pronoun use reflects standings in social hierarchies", JLSP 33(2), 125–143; checked 18 Nov from the abstract and secondary sources only, full text paywalled: five studies, status always relative to the others in the same interaction) suggest that higher-status people say "I" less and "we" more. That runs against the common idea that people at the top are more self-focused. We test this in unscripted speech, using NBA playoff press conferences, where status is public and objective.

- **Main question:** do superstars use proportionally more self-focused ("I") and less team-focused ("we") language than role players, after accounting for game result, points, minutes, the questions asked (wording and topic) and playoff round?
- **Supporting question:** apart from pronouns, which words most distinguish the groups, and is the difference mostly about pronouns?
- **Prediction:** superstars will use relatively **more "we"**. Test two-sided.
- **Scope:** NBA playoff press conferences only. Results are described as "consistent with" the status explanation, not proof of it.
- **Coaches are excluded (decided 8 Nov).** They are not on the status ladder, and speaking for the team is part of their job. No coach analysis and no coach reference bar. "Coaches after wins vs losses" goes on the future-work slide only. The players' win/loss effect is reported from the `won` coefficient in model A (compare with Cialdini et al., 1976, "basking in reflected glory", JPSP 34(3), 366–375; read in full 18 Nov: Exp. 2, 168 students by phone, "we" in 32% of win descriptions vs 18% of non-wins, χ²(1) = 4.20, p < .05. Fans, not players; our players show the opposite, more "we" after losses, so present it as a contrast).

**Step 7 LDA (done 8 Nov).**

  3. Fourth quarter and closing

## Limitations to state

- The controls reduce but do not remove other explanations ("consistent with", not proof).
- Stars may also differ in media training, age or personality. The same-player model addresses stable personality, not media training that changes over time.
- Playoff press conferences only, mostly the Finals and conference finals, so all teams are strong.
- Role-player game-day units are the smallest cell (302).
- The main comparison rules out a superstar vs role gap larger than ~4.5 points of the team-focused share (~4 pp of we-share), but not a smaller one.
- The main BERT measure rests on AI training labels; a blind human check of 100 random uses (Step 23) agrees 92% on "we" but only 66% on "I" (κ 0.43; 62% against the classifier), mostly on framing verbs ("I think"). It finds about half of the credit-giving "I" (24/46; misses Kobe's "not me"), and was made the main measure after a first analysis (both measures give the same answer).
- Teammate names are matched automatically, so some mentions are missed or wrongly counted.
- Role players reach post-game press conferences mainly after their own good games (+5.2 pts vs own average), so game-day comparisons are affected by selection.

## Gotchas and conventions

- **Basketball-Reference** returns 403 to python-requests and then blocks the IP for at least an hour. Do not use it. stats.nba.com through `nba_api` works with 2 s delays. The Wikipedia API needs serial requests at least 5 s apart.
- **ASAP pages vary by era.** Speaker labels can contain lowercase letters (`LeBRON JAMES:`, `McCOLLUM:`). There are several footer markers. Older pages are double-encoded UTF-8; `fix_text()` repairs this.
- **Name matching:** `key()` folds accents, punctuation and suffixes such as Jr. Spelling variants go in `ALIASES` (e.g. enes kanter → enes freedom, patrick mills → patty mills). Non-players go in `NON_PLAYERS`.
- **Coach detection:** a person counts as a coach from their first head-coaching season onwards, which handles Jason Kidd and Derek Fisher.
- Notebooks are generated by builder scripts and executed with `nbclient`. If you edit a notebook directly, re-run it top to bottom.
- Run scripts with `python -I` when they read downloaded pages. Code that needs user site-packages (nbformat, nba_api) runs without `-I`.
- Figure palette: superstar `#2a78d6` (blue), non-superstar `#eb6834` (orange). For three tiers, add `#1baf7a` (aqua, slot 3) and validate. Gridlines are recessive, and there's a legend whenever there are two or more groups.
- The user wants **brutally honest feedback** and works through problems one at a time.
